and a fail-closed audit. Outside repositories were cloned into folders outside this repo and **read only** (no scripts or builds run);

## Headline

- **No bug in the prayer arithmetic or the Hijri dates.** The launcher's `PrayerMath` and the page's `computePrayers` are within one minute of an

## 1. Prayer times

### Method

`tests/prayer_crosscheck_gen.mjs` takes the 374 cities of `Cities.java` (194 Saudi, 15 Omani, 24 Egyptian, 20 Moroccan, 18 Algerian, 18 Tunisian,
14 Jordanian, 13 Syrian, 11 Emirati, 9 Qatari, 9 Lebanese, 9+4 Palestinian, 8 Bahraini, 8 Kuwaiti), the method our table gives their zone, and writes

day, 1 Shawwal, both years) for six Saudi cities, and the Ja'fari method for six Saudi cities: **5 136 city-days, 30 816 times**. The reference
times are read as the city's wall clock at that instant (so a DST day is judged by the real clock, not by our noon-offset shortcut).

`tests/prayer_crosscheck_test.py` (launcher, `PrayerCrosscheckRun.java`) and `tests/prayer_crosscheck_page.mjs` (page, in Chromium, run by
`run.sh`) compare ours with it, one minute allowed. The test also checks that the file is exactly what the generator writes and that every city of

`tests/prayer_published.tsv` (48 days of timetables printed by the Makkah Umm al-Qura calendar, Qatar's Awqaf, the UAE's Awqaf and a Kuwaiti app,

### 1.1 Why Asr differs by one minute in 41% of rows (explained, and it is the reference that is off)

Without rounding, over all cities every third day of 2026–2027 (91 256 samples) the app is within **6.6 s (Fajr), 3.6 s (sunrise), 1.5 s (Dhuhr),
3.5 s (Maghrib), 14.5 s (Isha)** of adhan's Meeus series; Asr is up to **49 s** away. The cause is in adhan, not in our code: its Asr takes the sun's
declination at 00:00 UT of the date when it works out the shadow angle, while the app takes it at 13:00 local solar time. Solving Asr exactly (the
declination at the Asr moment itself) the app is within **12–26 s** (0°–55° north) and adhan within 36–115 s. Nothing to fix; the ±1 minute is
two roundings meeting. Below 1 minute it cannot become 2 at our latitudes: the largest Asr difference to adhan at any city is 49 s.

### 1.2 DST and clock-change days

Cairo (24 April and 29–30 October 2026, 29–30 April and 28–29 October 2027), Beirut (28–29 March, 24–25 October 2026; 27–28 March, 30–31
October 2027), Hebron and Gaza (27–28 March, 23–24 October 2026; 26–27 March, 29–30 October 2027), Casablanca (14–15 February and 21–22 March
2026; 6–7 February and 13–14 March 2027), plus Syria, Jordan (fixed +03 since 2022), Tunisia, Algeria and the Gulf (no change). All within one
minute. The test skips a row (and fails if more than 2% are skipped) when the machine's time-zone database disagrees with the one that wrote the
file; none were skipped here (Node ICU tz 2025b, JDK 21.0.12).

### 1.3 Ramadan (Umm al-Qura Isha +90 / +120)

Ramadan 1447 = 2026-02-18..03-19, 1448 = 2027-02-08..03-08 (the official table, task 2). The launcher gets its flag from the caller, the page from
Intl; the page flag equals the file's on all Saudi rows including the edges. Only Umm al-Qura gets +30, as documented; Qatar and the Gulf method keep
90.

### 1.4 High latitudes

No city in the lists is above 37.3° N (Bizerte), so none is in the files. A user-chosen place can be. Spot check with Umm al-Qura at Oslo (59.9° N),
London and Istanbul on the two solstices: the app equals adhan's «middle of the night» rule to the minute at Oslo (where our clamp and that rule
coincide at midsummer: 01:19 Fajr), and within one minute at London and Istanbul. Other seasons and latitudes above 48° were not examined.

### 1.5 Method parameters: what is confirmed

adhan has Umm al-Qura, Dubai, Qatar, Kuwait and Egyptian with exactly our angles, intervals and margins (Dubai −3/+3/+3/+3 included), and the printed
timetables agree for the first four. **Gulf (8), Jordan (23), Morocco (21), Algeria (19), Tunisia (18) and the Ja'fari are only as good as our
documentation**: the reference feeds the same numbers to the formulas, so it confirms the astronomy and the wiring, not the numbers. Aladhan's list
was not reachable. adhan adds +1 minute to Dhuhr for the Egyptian, ISNA, MWL and Karachi methods; we (and, as far as I know, Aladhan's id 5) do not.
I left it out of the reference; if the Egyptian authority's own timetable carries it, Egypt's Dhuhr is a minute early (unconfirmed, low).

## 4. 0x006E/cleantv's LAN protocol vs our phone control

Read: `docs/PROTOCOL.md`, `CryptoUtils.java`, `TvCommandServer.java` (Apache-2.0). Ours: `origin/work/phone-pin`, `android/launcher/.../home/phone/`
(`Pairing`, `Sessions`, `Guard`, `PhoneServer`, `PhoneApi`, `PinGate`, `PhoneActivity`).

| | cleantv | Ours |
|---|---|---|
| Transport | plain HTTP on every interface (`new ServerSocket()` bound to the port, CORS `*`); the body is AES-256-GCM | plain HTTP, bound to the Wi-Fi private address only, private clients only, Host/Origin/Sec-Fetch checks, no CORS; stated plainly in FEATURES.md |
| Pairing secret | 6-digit PIN from `java.util.Random`, valid 15 minutes, **reused for every pairing in that time**, written to logcat | 128-bit one-time token in the QR plus a 6-digit code, `SecureRandom`, 2 minutes, spent on first use; **the person presses «سماح» on the TV** |
| Wrong guesses | none counted (`validatePairingPin` has no counter); the encrypted path is an oracle that costs the TV a PBKDF2 per try | 5 wrong tries lock the whole pairing; constant-time compare; 12 pairing requests per minute per client |
| Key derivation | PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256, **10 000 iterations, fixed salt** (the doc names another salt than the code) from a 6-digit PIN: 10⁶ candidates, so a captured handshake gives up the PIN offline in minutes on a GPU | none: nothing is derived from a guessable secret |
| After pairing | a bearer token and a session key, **stored forever** in plain SharedPreferences; token also accepted in `?token=`; 120-second timestamp window, no nonce memory | 256-bit session in a `HttpOnly; SameSite=Strict` cookie **and** a header, bound to the phone's IP, 30 min idle / 4 h max, 5 sessions, nothing on disk |
| Revocation | per-device `unpair` (any holder of the token, no proof of ownership beyond it) and `unpairAll` | the owner's list on the TV with a cancel button per phone, stop-all, 5 wrong parental PINs end that session |
| PIN for changes | none | the parental PIN for `set` and `update install` through `PinGate` (the lockout is the TV's) |
| Unauthenticated surface | `/api/status` (TV name, provider, counts) | `/api/pair` pieces only; `/api/state` needs a session |

**Is there anything to adopt?** Not their cryptography. Encryption would be the only thing they have that we lack, and it cannot be had cheaply:
the phone's page is served over plain HTTP, where browsers do not offer `crypto.subtle` (secure contexts only), so AES-GCM or HMAC would mean shipping
a JavaScript crypto library in the page, and a key agreement from the 6-digit code would need a PAKE (a 20-bit secret through PBKDF2 is what
cleantv does and is the weakness). The token in the QR (128 bits) could seed an HMAC per request, but the threat it removes (someone on the same
Wi-Fi who can already spoof the phone's address *and* sniff) is small beside what the TV-side approval, IP binding and 30-minute sessions already
stop, and FEATURES.md tells the family the truth about it.

Ranked ideas:

1. **Keep the design; keep the plain-HTTP warning prominent** (it is in FEATURES.md and on the TV screen). Value: keeps trust. Effort 0.
2. **Optional «تذكّر هذا الجوال»**: a long-lived, revocable device key (stored as a hash, rotated at each use, listed with its date in the TV's
   «الجوالات المتصلة»), so a family does not scan the QR every four hours. cleantv's persistent tokens are the pattern, minus its flaws (store a hash,
   expire after 30–90 days, never accept it in a URL). Value medium (convenience), effort M, risk medium (a stolen key lasts longer; the IP binding must
   stay or become a soft check since home addresses change).
3. **Auth-failure log with no secrets** shown on the TV («محاولة ربط فاشلة قبل دقيقة»). Value low–medium, effort S, risk low. cleantv logs the PIN and token to
   logcat: ours must keep logging neither (a `grep -n "Log\." phone/*.java` finds none today).
4. Do not adopt: PIN-derived keys, query-string tokens, `*` CORS, PIN reuse, unauthenticated status.
