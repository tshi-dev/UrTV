# How the apps fetch their data files

`Net` (the same file in both apps) is the one small GET for the apps' own data: IPv4 addresses first, 4 s to connect to each, a deadline for the whole call, a size cap, no redirect, https by the platform's TLS with the host name checked. It exists because `HttpURLConnection` tries every address of a host in turn with the whole connect timeout, IPv6 first: a TV whose IPv6 goes nowhere waited 4 x 12 s for raw.githubusercontent.com (the leagues list took 54 s on a real TV). `tests/net_test.py` runs it on a plain JVM against a scripted server and, where there is a way out (CI), over real https.

Through Net now: the leagues list (SettingsActivity), the quiz bank, the next match (the hub), the home's update notice (version.txt), the markets' data files (MarketFile.download).

Still on HttpURLConnection, with their connect timeouts (the worst wait is the number of addresses times the timeout; a big download is not a Net job: it streams to a file): the screensaver's update checks and downloads (Updater 10 to 15 s), the photo packs and the page's photos and fonts (PackStore, PhotoCache 15 s), the transliteration lookup (Arabic 10 s), the settings' searches (cities, shows: SettingsActivity.fetch 12 s), the live prices (MarketFile.live 10 s, which also checks the redirected host), the home's weather and place lookups (Greeting 6 s), the prayer times (PrayerTimes 6 s, Adhan 8 s), the mirror checks and downloads of the suggested apps (SuggestedAppsActivity 10 s), the phone updater (PhoneUpdater 10 s), the setup's downloads (SetupApks, SetupHost 10 s), the home's wallpaper photos (MainActivity 10 s), watch-next pictures (TvRows). The next steps, in this order: the small JSON ones (settings' searches, weather, prayer times, the updaters' version checks) go through Net; for the streaming ones the connect is the only part that matters, so a `Net.connect(host, port)` that returns the connected socket the same way (IPv4 first, 4 s) is the way to give them the same fix.
